workflows. Each runs its own fresh waw-native DFT (same convention as
`workflows/w90tutorial/`, no precomputed fixture) and produces the figures

| `24_lova_transport.ipynb` | **Electrical resistivity from $\alpha^2F_{\rm tr}$** (NEW capability, `analysis.elph_boltzmann` + `elph.alpha2f_transport_matrix` + `band_sheets.sheet_drude_weight`) — Allen's lowest-order variational solution of the linearised Boltzmann equation (*Phys. On **MgB$_2$** ($\sigma$/$\pi$) band resolution raises $\sigma_{ab}$ by **20–30%** where on Al it changes nothing (1 part in $10^6$) — the result the two-sheet treatment exists for — with $\rho_{ab}(300\,$K$)$ in the measured 5–10 $\mu\Omega\,$cm crystal range; the predicted $\rho_c/\rho_{ab} < 1$ contradicts experiment and is flagged, not explained. Verifies the kernel's two analytic limits numerically (high-$T$ slope $2\pi\lambda_{\rm tr}k_BT$; Bloch-Grüneisen $T^5$, the exponent a self-energy relaxation time gets wrong as $T^3$)  Extended with **Nb** and **Co**, and every Fermi level is now the *model's own* rather than the SCF one (`fermi_level_from_electron_count`, and `fermi_level_spin_channels` for the collinear pair) — on Nb the QE value is 40 meV low and holds 4.94 electrons in the 6-orbital s+d manifold instead of 5, which inflates $N(E_F)$ by 6–8%. Nb is the strong-coupling end ($\lambda = 1.18$ vs a literature 1.0–1.3 and a specific-heat 1.22, where Al is 0.36): $\rho$ comes out 1.6× low **at both 77 K and 300 K**, so it is a prefactor. Two independent constraints then localise it. The specific heat contains no plasma frequency and predicts $\lambda$ from $N(E_F)$ alone: at the SCF Fermi level that route and $\alpha^2F$ disagree by 13%, at the model's own they agree to a few percent, so $N(E_F)$ is pinned and the error is **not** in the DOS. And the narrowing scan *refutes* a rigid bandwidth correction — $N(E_F)\sim1/f$ forces $\lambda\sim1/f$ alongside $\rho\sim1/f^2$, so reaching the measured 14.5 needs $f<0.80$ where $\lambda$ is already 1.46 against 1.22. The surviving target for GW is therefore specific: lower the Fermi velocities while leaving $N(E_F)$ alone, which only a band-selective ($s$ vs $d$) correction can do — subject to confirming that the literature $\Omega_p\approx9.5$ eV is the *bare* value and not a mass-enhanced Drude fit, since a dressed 9.5 implies a bare 14.2 and reverses the sign. Co exercises `spin_resolved_conductivity` (two-current Fert–Campbell, `num_elec_per_state` = 1 per channel and it does **not** cancel): spin asymmetry $\alpha\approx21$ with the majority channel carrying 95% of the current, but $\rho(300$ K$)$ spans 1.26–2.05 $\mu\Omega\,$cm over $20^3$–$36^3$ because that majority sheet has $N(E_F)\approx0.18$ /eV/cell — mesh-limited, ~3× below the measured hcp 6.2, with fcc-vs-hcp, magnon scattering and spin mixing all absent. Co is also the material that exposed the UPF $j$-averaging bug (`fe0ce4a`) | reuses notebooks 13 (Al), 16 (MgB$_2$), plus fresh Nb and Co el-ph runs |

| `27_dmft_mott_transition.ipynb` | **The Mott transition, with a real impurity solver** (NEW capability, `analysis.dmft` — exact diagonalisation of an Anderson impurity in a Lehmann representation, plus Hubbard-I and IPT, on the Bethe lattice where the self-consistency is the one-line $\Delta = t^2 G$). Pinned first against everything that is known exactly: the atomic limit to $10^{-13}$, $U = 0$ to $4\times10^{-15}$, particle-hole symmetry, the $\Sigma$ tail, Padé continuation to $3\times10^{-13}$. Then the transition **from both sides**, which is the point — the metallic and insulating branches are both stable in between, so a single sweep finds a crossing rather than a phase boundary. Coexistence widens monotonically as $T$ falls (0.00 / 0.45 / 0.65 / 1.00 over $T = 0.02$–$0.0025\,D$) and brackets NRG's $T=0$ values $U_{c1} = 2.38$, $U_{c2} = 2.94$. Three traps, all of which produced plausible wrong answers first: the self-consistency map has a Jacobian eigenvalue near 2 near the transition, where linear mixing is **provably** powerless at any mixing parameter (fixed with Anderson/DIIS); a convergence test that accepts one sub-tolerance iteration is satisfied by an oscillating map (fixed with `n_stable=3`); and the spinodals are **not bit-reproducible run to run**, so an estimator that takes the first crossing of a branch can be flipped by one stray point — it reported $U_{c1} > U_{c2}$, an unphysical negative coexistence width, until it was changed to take branch extrema. IPT is certified against ED by the scaling of their difference, $\max\lvert\Delta\Sigma\rvert \propto U^{4.01}$, i.e. they agree to $O(U^3)$ exactly as second-order perturbation theory must; it puts $U_{c2}$ above ED's, the expected direction for a second-order solver, and the two bracket a number neither can reach. Closes with the same model under a static Hubbard $U$ (the DFT+U analogue), whose gap opens at $U = 1$ where DMFT is still a good metal ($Z = 0.70$) — a different phase held open by a moment DMFT never needs, not an early Mott transition | self-contained (no DFT input) |
| `28_elph_band_renormalisation.ipynb` | **What phonons do to the bands, not to the carriers** (NEW capability, `analysis.elph_selfenergy` Fan-Migdal + `analysis.spectral`, a Sigma-agnostic Bloch spectral function now shared with `analysis.cpa`). The complement of notebooks 13/24: the same validated vertex $g(\mathbf R_e,\mathbf R_q)$, contracted into a frequency-dependent self-energy instead of a collision integral. **Validated on two materials with opposite physics** by a test that shares the vertex and nothing else — $\lambda$ from $-\partial{\rm Re}\Sigma/\partial\omega$ against $\lambda$ from $2\int\alpha^2F/\omega$: Al 0.475 vs 0.482 (1.5%, and $\alpha^2F$'s value lands on notebook 13's converged $0.478\pm0.009$), Nb 1.187 vs 1.208 (1.7%, against a specific-heat 1.22). The residual is the double-delta approximation measured, and it is **not** the DOS slope — Al's free-electron continuum and Nb's $d$-band shoulder give the same 1.5-1.7%. **Phonons narrow Al's bands by 32% at $E_F$** ($Z = 0.678$), a far larger renormalisation than the 15-20% notebook 24 scanned for Nb — and it changes $\rho$ by nothing, because the velocity and the DOS are renormalised by reciprocal factors, which is *why* the LOVA formula takes the bare $\Omega_p$. Debye-Waller omitted deliberately: static, so it touches neither $Z$ nor the kink nor ${\rm Im}\,\Sigma$ | reuses notebooks 13 (Al) and 24 (Nb) el-ph runs |


## Running

```bash
uv pip install nbformat nbconvert ipykernel jupyterlab   # once
python -m ipykernel install --user --name waw      # register the venv kernel
jupyter lab                                               # then open a notebook
```

The notebooks locate the repo root automatically (walk up to the `waw`
package), so they run from anywhere.

Regenerate the notebooks (code cells only) with `python _generate.py`, or pass
a name substring (e.g. `python _generate.py fe110`) to rebuild just one without
wiping the others' executed outputs.
