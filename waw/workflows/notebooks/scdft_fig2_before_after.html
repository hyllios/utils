Why it moved

Not a missing term, which is what the monotonic climb looked like. Both SCDFT kernels have power-law tails in ξ′, so their energy integrals converge only as 1/L in the window L. The old default L = 30 ωmax was far from converged, and the error is λ-dependent — +3.0% at λ = 0.4 rising to +6.4% at λ = 2.8. A λ-dependent convergence error is indistinguishable from a missing term.

What is left is 2–3% with no λ trend, and the window absorbs it: a ~3 eV electronic window reproduces the published curve to 0.55% rms. The Letter does not state its window, so this cannot be pinned down further.

Addendum (2026-07-31): the residual is the paper's, not ours

The closing sentence above is now refuted. The raw data behind the Letter's Fig. 1 (pdf/fig1.agr, from Antonio) runs on a logarithmic ξ grid out to ±27 eV — the authors' own electronic window, which matches waw's converged one. At matched windows the published SCDFT curve sits 2–3.5% above these equations, and a ~3 eV window is not something their calculation used.

Three findings settle where that residual lives. First, waw's kernels now agree to 10 digits, pointwise and at T = 0, with SCTK (github.com/mitsuaki1987/sctk, scdft_kernel = 2) — a third, fully independent implementation whose kernels are symbolic Matsubara closed forms. Three independent derivations of Eqs. (10)–(11) agree exactly; any correct solver of the printed theory must produce waw's numbers. Second, the constants are exactly the printed ones and cannot absorb the residual: A. Sanna confirmed (2026-07-31) that his code runs γ = 1.330, 3.800, 1.33000, and that γ₃ affects only the gap, not T₃ — a structural property waw shares, since γ₃ drops out of the linearized T₃ identically (verified: 78.8609 K for γ₃ = 1.33, 1.95 and 5.0 alike). An earlier version of this page proposed that the printed 3.8 was a rounding of SCTK's 3.88; that is retracted — 3.88 is SCTK's own value. Third, the authors' own two publications disagree with each other by the same margin as with waw: at λ = 2, μ = 0 the ratio is 0.993 in PRL 125 but 0.979 in the Nature Rev. Phys. (2024) SI model, in the opposite order to what their two windows imply (waw: 0.963 and 0.965, nearly equal as they must be).

Conclusion: no bug in waw, and with the constants pinned by their author the remaining 1.5–2.8% is unexplained but provably outside these kernels. At the confirmed γ's, waw/published runs 0.976, 0.980, 0.985, 0.967, 0.968 across λ = 0.4–2.8. Closing it by fitting would need γ₁ ≈ 1.34–1.35, which those values exclude, so the candidates all lie beyond the printed equations: their electronic window for Fig. 2 specifically, their Eliashberg reference, or their numerics.
